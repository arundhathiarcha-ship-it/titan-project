What's actually happening under the hood

A genuine, functioning computer vision pipeline — in service of a number nobody needs.

1

RGB → HSV conversion

Every pixel is converted to hue/saturation/value so color can be judged independently of lighting and shine.

2

Hue thresholding

Pixels inside a narrow olive-green hue band, dark and matte enough to be a cooked leaf rather than a fresh backdrop, are flagged as candidates.

3

Binary mask

The image collapses into black and white: white pixels are "maybe a leaf."

4

Flood-fill labeling

A breadth-first search groups touching white pixels into connected blobs, the same way MS Paint's bucket tool works.

5

Size + shape filtering

Tiny specks get discarded, and large, sparse, sprawling blobs — the telltale shape of a banana-leaf backdrop rather than a compact curry leaf — get rejected even if their pixel count alone would pass.

6

Centroid overlay

Each surviving blob gets a circle drawn at its center, and the final count is reported with total, unearned confidence.

Runs 100% client-side. No image ever leaves your browser — mostly because no server would want it.
